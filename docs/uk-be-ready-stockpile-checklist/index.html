UK Be Ready Checklist: Why Beans and Water Are Selling Out

Ministers say there is no specific threat behind the advice, but the rush on tinned food and torches shows how quickly a calm government message can turn into empty shelves.

  • By Uptick Desk
  • Published Oct. 28, 2026, 11:52 a.m. ET
  • About 5 min read
  • Reviewed by the site owner on Oct. 28, 2026
  • 5 sources

Quick take

  • The Cabinet Office’s Be Ready campaign, published Oct. 22, 2026, and set to run for three years, asks households to be able to manage on their own for three days.
  • The checklist suggests 3 liters of drinking water per person per day, food that needs no cooking, a torch, a wind-up or battery radio, cash and copies of documents.
  • Iceland reported tinned food sales up 40%, Robert Dyas said radio sales rose 160% and Ocado ran out of several bottled waters and baked beans.
  • Defence Secretary Wes Streeting says there is no specific threat and has urged people to be ‘alert but not alarmed.’

What happened

British shoppers have been clearing shelves of tinned food, bottled water, torches and radios since the government published new emergency guidance last week. The campaign, called Be Ready, asks every household to be able to look after itself for three days if normal services break down.

The Cabinet Office put out an updated website and a downloadable Be Ready checklist on Thursday, Oct. 22, according to a Sky News report carried by Radio X. Prime Minister Andy Burnham promoted it the next day at a summit on wildfires and resilience, Arab News reported. By the weekend, retailers were reporting gaps on their shelves, and “beans and water sell out” became one of the top searches in the U.K. on Google Trends.

What’s on the Be Ready checklist

The list is practical rather than dramatic. ITV News set out the main groups:

What The guidance
Water 3 liters a day for each person to drink, with more set aside for cooking and washing
Food Three days of non-perishable food that needs no fridge or cooking, ideally things you already eat
Light and warmth A torch with spare batteries, blankets, sleeping bags, warm clothes
News A wind-up or battery FM radio and a charged power bank
Money and papers Cash in notes and coins, copies of ID, key phone numbers on paper, and a note of any medicines and their doses
Health and hygiene First aid kit, essential medicines, hand sanitizer, wet wipes
Babies and pets Formula, nappies and wipes; pet food and water

The guidance also suggests building the kit slowly, adding a few items on each shopping trip, and checking expiry dates every six months. Neighbors are encouraged to swap contact details.

The numbers behind the advice

The water figure is the one that surprises people. Three days at 3 liters a day comes to 9 liters per person, which is the “about nine litres” figure LBC reported. For a family of four, that’s 36 liters just for drinking, or eighteen 2-liter bottles, before any water for cooking or washing. Multiply that by millions of households acting in the same few days, and it’s easy to see why online grocers ran short.

Why it’s happening now

Ministers say this is routine preparation, not a response to a specific danger. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said there is no particular threat behind it and called it “everyday national resilience,” ITV reported. He has urged people to be “alert but not alarmed.”

The risks the government lists are broad: storms and flooding, wildfires and drought, cyberattacks, and disruption to supply chains or payment systems. Streeting framed it as a simple question: “How would we cope if the payment systems at the local supermarket went down?” Arab News linked the timing to recent events including a cyberattack that made refrigerators overheat in hundreds of French supermarkets and a serious flaw found in PHP, the programming language that runs much of the web. The National noted that the guidance also landed just before two named storms, Austen and Boelo, hit the U.K. that weekend.

The campaign is set to run for three years and replaces an earlier version launched in 2024, according to the Sky report.

How Britain compares with Europe

The U.K. is following, not leading. Since 2024, France, Germany, Czechia, Denmark, the Netherlands, Poland, Belgium and the three Baltic states have all published household preparation guides, ITV reported, and Ireland launched its own Be Emergency Ready campaign in August. Security Minister Dan Jarvis said the three-day message puts the U.K. in line with those European neighbors. Streeting went further, saying European countries are “way ahead of us on some of this stuff.”

Some in the resilience sector had expected a bigger launch, according to the Sky report, which noted that Nordic and Baltic countries have long given their citizens far more detailed advice.

What the shops are reporting

Retailer What it reported
Iceland Tinned food sales up 40%; says its supplies remain healthy
Ocado Several bottled waters, baked beans and some soups out of stock; working to restore supply
Robert Dyas Radio sales up 160% on the previous week
Maplin Torch sales nearly quadrupled and radio sales almost doubled in three days
Preppers Shop UK Sales of “off-grid” items up 650%

Figures from The National and LBC. The National also pointed out that sales of these goods usually climb in storm season anyway.

What happens next

Retailers are restocking: Iceland says its supplies remain healthy, and Ocado says it is working with its suppliers to bring items back. The government is also pushing people who may need extra help in an emergency to join the Priority Services Register, which lets energy, water and gas companies know who might need support during an outage, LBC reported. The Be Ready site links to local Community Risk Registers and Met Office alerts so people can see which hazards matter most where they live.

Quick answers

Is the UK government warning of a specific emergency?

No. Ministers say there is no particular threat behind the campaign and describe it as general preparedness for things like storms, power cuts and cyberattacks.

How much water does the Be Ready checklist recommend?

Three liters of drinking water a day for each person, for three days. That adds up to about 9 liters each, with more needed for cooking and washing.

Do I need to buy everything at once?

No. The guidance suggests building up supplies gradually on normal shopping trips and checking dates every six months.
