Brazil Runoff Results: Lula Wins a 4th Term With 51.8%

Lula turned a 2.2 million-vote first-round deficit into a lead of about 4.4 million, the first comeback of its kind in Brazil’s modern elections. His opponent says he will go to court.

  • By Uptick Desk
  • Updated Oct. 29, 2026, 10:20 a.m. ET
  • About 8 min read
  • Reviewed by the site owner on Oct. 29, 2026
  • 14 sources

Quick take

  • With all votes counted, Lula won 51.8% of valid votes to Flávio Bolsonaro’s 48.2%, a margin of nearly 4.4 million votes, according to the electoral court figures reported by the AP.
  • Lula is the first candidate since Brazil’s return to direct presidential elections to win after finishing second in the first round.
  • Bolsonaro said he will seek to have the vote annulled over a two-hour extension of voting hours; the electoral court’s president said the extension did not change the result.
  • Lula’s fourth term begins Jan. 5, 2027.
  • Bolsonaro left Brazil despite a Supreme Court order to hand over his passports and reached Miami on Oct. 28; his wife and daughters were granted U.S. humanitarian parole.

Why it's trending Brazil’s electoral court declared President Lula the winner of the Oct. 25 runoff, and Flávio Bolsonaro refused to accept the result.

Latest: Bolsonaro leaves Brazil for Miami

Three days after losing the runoff, Flávio Bolsonaro was in Florida. He, his wife and their two daughters landed at Miami-Opa Locka Executive Airport at about 9:30 a.m. local time on Wednesday, Oct. 28, Al Jazeera reported.

He left despite a court order. Shortly after the election, Justice Alexandre de Moraes of Brazil’s Supreme Court told him to surrender his passports within 24 hours and not to leave the country, citing flight risk, TIME reported. The order is tied to a money laundering investigation that grew out of a federal probe into unauthorized deductions from pensioners’ payments. Bolsonaro denies wrongdoing and called the order an attempt to intimidate him. The deadline passed, and both his ordinary and his diplomatic passports were then canceled by Brazilian authorities, Bloomberg reported.

The route, as reconstructed by The Wall Street Journal:

When (local time) What happened
Mon., Oct. 26, evening A convoy of four cars leaves his Brasília home and splits up
Tue., Oct. 27, 10:50 p.m. The family crosses a bridge into Puerto Iguazú, Argentina
Wed., Oct. 28, 12:21 a.m. A chartered jet takes off, refueling in Guayaquil, Ecuador
Wed., Oct. 28, about 9:30 a.m. The plane lands near Miami

The U.S. role: President Trump personally approved letting the family in, the Journal reported. The State Department said it would issue visas, but his wife and children entered under humanitarian parole, Bloomberg reported, a status the Journal says allows a one-year stay. U.S. border records show he used his canceled diplomatic passport, according to Bloomberg. Trump called the family “fine people,” per Al Jazeera, and said he “won’t get involved,” per the Journal.

Brazil’s reaction: Lula called the flight “a shameful act,” TIME reported. Bolsonaro said “the system won” and that he would organize opposition in Congress. More than 50 countries have recognized Lula’s victory, the Journal reported; Trump has not, and has suggested without evidence that the result was rigged.

This mirrors his father’s path. After losing in 2022, Jair Bolsonaro also went to Florida, staying about three months. Flávio now plans to run a political operation from the U.S., the Journal reported.

What happened

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil’s presidential runoff on Sunday, Oct. 25, taking 51.8% of valid votes to 48.2% for Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro. With every vote counted, the Superior Electoral Court declared Lula the winner by nearly 4.4 million votes, the Associated Press reported. At 80, he now heads into a fourth, non-consecutive term.

Bolsonaro did not concede. Speaking Sunday night, he said the result could not be recognized and that his side would go to court to have the election annulled, Bloomberg reported. His complaint centers on a Supreme Court justice’s order to keep some polls open two extra hours. The head of the electoral court said that extension did not affect the outcome.

How Lula came back

Two weeks earlier, Lula looked like the underdog. Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, won the Oct. 4 first round with 47.03% to Lula’s 45.16%, Anadolu Agency noted. Final polls called the runoff a toss-up; Datafolha had it at 50% each, The Rio Times’ tracker showed.

No one had won the presidency from second place in the first round since Brazil returned to direct presidential elections, according to the AP and Anadolu. Lula did it by going after people who skipped the first round and voters who hadn’t decided, and by leaning on his Northeast stronghold, Bloomberg wrote in its results story. In the campaign’s last stretch he raised Bolsa Família payments, banned online gambling and announced a plan to refinance consumer debt through private banks.

The count itself was a seesaw. Bolsonaro led for part of the night and peaked at 50.30% with about 37% of ballots counted, Latin Times reported from court updates. Lula retook the lead once returns from the Northeast and big cities came in and never gave it back.

What the math says

  • The swing: Bolsonaro went from a lead of about 2.22 million votes to a deficit of about 4.43 million. That is a turnaround of roughly 6.65 million votes in three weeks.
  • Where it came from: Lula added about 10.4 million votes between rounds, nearly three times Bolsonaro’s gain of 3.7 million. His campaign’s push to bring first-round no-shows to the polls appears to have paid off: O Povo reported that runoff abstention fell to 19%, the lowest in 20 years.
  • The map: Each man carried 14 of the 28 voting units counted by Poliwave, which include the 26 states, the Federal District and votes cast abroad. Bolsonaro won São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and the South; Lula swept the Northeast, winning Bahia by about 43 points. The closest state was Minas Gerais, which Lula won by less than a point.

Why Bolsonaro wants the vote annulled

The dispute is over the last hour of voting. Lula’s campaign asked for more time after bus shortages in Salvador and São Paulo kept people from reaching polls, and Bolsonaro’s campaign asked for more time in states hit by heavy rain, according to the AP and O Povo. With the 5 p.m. closing time almost at hand, Justice Cristiano Zanin ordered them kept open until 7 p.m. He later clarified that the order covered only polling places still open, and many had already closed, Bloomberg reported.

Bolsonaro accused Zanin, who once worked as Lula’s lawyer, of changing the rules mid-game. “It’s impossible to recognize the results,” he said, per the AP.

What officials say: Electoral court president Kassio Nunes Marques, who was appointed by Jair Bolsonaro, congratulated both candidates and said the extension delayed the count but did not change the result, and that there was no outside interference, Bloomberg reported. He said the transport problems would be investigated.

What the law allows: A candidate can request a recount with evidence of fraud, the AP noted. Law professor João Pedro Pádua told the AP there is little legal basis for annulment unless the extension can be shown to have kept voters from freely expressing their will.

The episode echoes 2022, when Jair Bolsonaro did not accept his narrow loss to Lula. He is now serving 27 years for a coup attempt that followed that election, the AP said.

What happens next

  • Jan. 5, 2027: Lula’s fourth term begins, according to the AP, Bloomberg and Anadolu.
  • Jan. 31, 2027: Flávio Bolsonaro’s Senate term ends, and with it certain protections from prosecution, TIME reported. Brazil and the U.S. have an extradition treaty, Al Jazeera noted, but its reporter said a quick handover is unlikely.
  • The court fight: Bolsonaro said he will file a legal challenge. Watch for the electoral court’s response.
  • Markets: Investors had hoped a Bolsonaro win would bring tighter public finances. An exchange-traded fund tracking Brazilian stocks fell 13% in Japan after the result, Bloomberg reported, and analysts said investors want a credible plan to rein in debt.
  • Governing: Lula has promised to bring the budget in line without cutting his social programs. Analyst Creomar de Souza told Bloomberg he “will need to be able to build a coalition.”

Quick answers

Who won Brazil’s 2026 presidential election?

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, with 51.8% of valid votes in the Oct. 25 runoff against Flávio Bolsonaro, who received 48.2%.

Did Flávio Bolsonaro concede?

No. He said he would seek to annul the runoff over the extension of voting hours. The electoral court’s president said the extension did not affect the result.

Where is Flávio Bolsonaro now?

In the Miami area. He arrived Oct. 28 after leaving Brazil through Argentina and Ecuador, despite a Supreme Court order to surrender his passports.

When does Lula’s new term start?

Jan. 5, 2027. It will be his fourth term; he also served from 2003 to 2010 and since 2023.
