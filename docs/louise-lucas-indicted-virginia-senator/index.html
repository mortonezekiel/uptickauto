Louise Lucas Indicted: Virginia Senator Faces Marijuana Charges

One of the most powerful Democrats in Richmond is accused of running a marijuana business from behind her budget gavel. She says she won’t back down, and the case lands 12 days before the midterms.

  • By Uptick Desk
  • Published Oct. 22, 2026, 6:15 p.m. ET
  • About 6 min read
  • Reviewed by the site owner on Oct. 27, 2026
  • 7 sources

Quick take

  • A federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Virginia returned an eight-count indictment against Lucas, 82, and six other defendants; it was unsealed Oct. 22.
  • Prosecutors allege the group handled 100 kilograms or more of marijuana, laundered the proceeds and used her Finance Committee chairmanship for protection.
  • Lucas surrendered, appeared in federal court in Norfolk and was released the same day. She said on X she would not be intimidated.
  • Fox News reports her arraignment is set for Nov. 16 in Newport News. The charges are allegations; no one has been convicted.

Why it's trending The Justice Department unsealed an eight-count federal indictment on Oct. 22 that accuses Virginia Senate President Pro Tempore Louise Lucas of leading a marijuana distribution enterprise.

What happened

Louise Lucas, the 82-year-old Democrat who presides over the Virginia Senate as president pro tempore and chairs its budget committee, was charged in a federal indictment unsealed on Thursday, Oct. 22. The Justice Department says the eight counts accuse seven people of taking part in a racketeering conspiracy built around selling marijuana.

Among the alleged crimes: selling marijuana, laundering money, bribery-based honest services fraud, wire fraud, keeping a property for drug activity, and having guns in connection with drug trafficking. CBS News and Fox News report that Lucas herself faces five of the eight counts.

Lucas turned herself in on Thursday morning and made a first appearance in federal court in Norfolk that afternoon. She was out of custody by later in the day, according to WTKR. Every charge is an allegation. As the Justice Department’s own release puts it, an indictment is “merely an allegation.”

What prosecutors allege

Federal prosecutors describe a group they call the “Lucas Enterprise,” which they say Lucas led. Their main claims:

  • Marijuana: the group allegedly arranged to buy and sell 100 kilograms or more of marijuana, roughly 220 pounds, and laundered the money.
  • Her office: Lucas allegedly used her position as chair of the Senate’s budget-writing Finance and Appropriations panel to keep the business away from state scrutiny and to steer legislation that helped it.
  • A $750,000 stake: in August 2022 she allegedly paid $750,000 for full ownership of a company tied to a recreational marijuana license that a co-defendant expected to get, and did not list it on her state financial disclosure forms.
  • A meeting at the Capitol: prosecutors say she met a co-conspirator in her office in the Capitol complex about a bribery payment linked to that license.

CBS reports that the 45-page indictment quotes Lucas describing a recreational license as a path to “generational wealth.” The other defendants include two of her daughters, a grandson and a business partner, the Justice Department says. Uptick is not naming them.

Two legal terms, explained

Racketeering charges let prosecutors treat a series of alleged crimes as the work of one ongoing organization. A local defense attorney told WTKR that this gives the government room to bring many allegations together, and that it doesn’t have to prove every single act if it establishes the overall scheme.

Honest services fraud is a federal charge for public officials accused of trading their official duties for private benefit, such as bribes. It’s why a case that starts with marijuana sales also becomes a corruption case.

AP reports that the investigation used a court-approved wiretap, and that it began during the Biden administration. The Virginia Mercury adds that agents found marijuana and firearms in the May searches.

Who says what

Lucas responded on X: “I will not be intimidated. I will not be bullied.” Brian Woolfolk, the top lawyer for a fund paying for her defense, called the case “the circus we always expected it to be,” AP reported.

Attorney General Todd Blanche rejected the idea that the case is political payback, according to AP. He told The Hill the investigation predated President Trump’s return to office.

Virginia Democrats questioned the timing. Senate Majority Leader Scott Surovell said Lucas is innocent until proven guilty, the Virginia Mercury reported. According to WTKR, Gov. Abigail Spanberger said Lucas and her family deserve the chance to defend themselves and that bringing charges this close to an election hurts trust in the Justice Department. Lt. Gov. Gazala Hashmi called for a thorough, nonpartisan investigation. House Republicans said the case should be settled in court.

Why it matters beyond Portsmouth

Lucas is one of the most influential lawmakers in Virginia. As budget chair she helped sink former Gov. Glenn Youngkin’s $2 billion plan to move Washington’s NBA and NHL teams to Virginia, AP notes, and she led the redistricting push that voters approved in April before the courts threw it out.

Timing is the other reason this is drawing attention. The charges came 12 days before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, though Lucas isn’t on the ballot. Her current Senate term runs until 2027, according to CBS. It wasn’t clear Thursday whether she would keep her leadership posts, the Mercury reported.

The case also lands as Virginia prepares to open legal recreational marijuana sales, which AP says are scheduled to begin in July 2027.

What happens next

Prosecutors did not ask to keep Lucas in custody, and she was released on her own recognizance, according to Fox News. Fox reports that her arraignment, where defendants usually enter a plea, is set for Nov. 16 in federal court in Newport News. No source we reviewed listed the maximum penalties she could face.

Quick answers

What is Louise Lucas charged with?

She is accused in a federal racketeering case involving marijuana distribution, money laundering and bribery-related fraud. CBS and Fox say she faces five of the eight counts in the indictment.

Has Louise Lucas responded?

Yes. In a post on X she said she won’t be intimidated, and a lawyer for her defense fund called the case a circus.

Is Louise Lucas still a state senator?

Yes. Being charged does not remove her from office, and her term runs until 2027.
