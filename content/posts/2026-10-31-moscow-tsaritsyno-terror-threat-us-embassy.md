## What happened

The US Embassy in Moscow issued a security alert on Saturday, Oct. 31, saying it was aware of a "credible threat of a mass casualty event" that same day at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve, a palace and park complex in southern Moscow, [AFP reports](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/russia-says-responding-us-report-160743844.html). The embassy said the US government was coordinating with Russian authorities and told people to stay away from the site and avoid crowds.

Russia's Federal Security Service, the FSB, confirmed it had received information from the United States about a planned terrorist attack in Moscow. It said it was taking appropriate measures but gave no details, according to the state news agency TASS as cited by [the Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/us-warns-russia-of-mass-casualty-event-threat-at-historic-palace-outside-moscow/).

By the evening, the reserve had been temporarily closed and access restricted, [Radio Moldova reports](https://radiomoldova.md/p/88634/moscow-closes-tsaritsyno-park-after-us-terror-warning), citing a Reuters report based on the RIA news agency. No attack had been reported in the coverage reviewed for this explainer.

## Why this alert stands out

Most travel warnings speak in general terms about a city or a country. This one named a single place, a single day and used the phrase "mass casualty," and it was picked up within hours by Russian media and by outlets in Ukraine, Turkey, Britain and the United States.

It also echoes a warning many readers remember. On March 7, 2024, the same embassy warned that extremists were planning, soon, to strike crowded events in the capital, concerts among them, and told Americans to avoid crowds for 48 hours, [IBTimes UK notes](https://ibtimes.co.uk/us-embassy-moscow-tsaritsyno-threat-warning-1829292). On March 22, gunmen attacked the Crocus City Hall concert venue outside Moscow and killed more than 140 people. A branch of the Islamic State claimed responsibility, and US officials later said Washington had tipped off Russia's security services beforehand.

There is no public evidence linking the new alert to the 2024 attack, and officials have not named a suspect or a group.

## Two warnings compared

| | March 2024 | October 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Date of US alert | March 7 | Oct. 31 |
| Target described | Large gatherings in Moscow, including concerts | One named site, the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve |
| Time window | Next 48 hours | "Today," Oct. 31 |
| Russian response | Officials later called the US information too general | FSB confirmed receipt and said it is acting |
| What followed | Crocus City Hall attack 15 days later | Reserve temporarily closed; no attack reported |

The difference in detail is the main change. A warning tied to one address and one date gives local police something concrete to act on, which a general warning about "large gatherings" did not.

## What is Tsaritsyno?

Tsaritsyno is an 18th-century estate built for Empress Catherine II, now a museum with a large surrounding park. It is one of Moscow's busier attractions, especially on weekends. Moscow deputy mayor Natalya Sergunina said in 2023 that more than 2 million people visited in 2022, [Fox News reports](https://www.foxnews.com/world/us-embassy-warns-credible-mass-casualty-threat-popular-moscow-tourist-attraction), and residents use the grounds for walks and exercise.

## What we don't know

The embassy gave no word on who could be responsible, what kind of attack was feared or how the information was obtained. Early Russian reports on the site's status conflicted. Security guards told one outlet the reserve was shut "for technical reasons," while a museum representative told another that staff knew of no threat, according to [Meduza](https://meduza.io/amp/en/news/2026/10/31/u-s-embassy-warns-of-mass-casualty-threat-at-moscow-s-tsaritsyno-museum-fsb-confirms-receiving-the-warning). A Telegram channel with reported law enforcement ties said the park might stay closed for two days, which officials had not confirmed.

The alert landed during a tense week in the war in Ukraine. Fox News reports that a Ukrainian strike on a refinery in southern Russia on Oct. 30 broke a two-week, Trump-brokered pause in attacks on energy sites. Kyiv has strongly denied past Russian claims, made without evidence, that it was involved in the Crocus attack.

## What happens next

- **The park's status:** watch for an official word from Moscow authorities on when Tsaritsyno reopens.
- **FSB statements:** the security service may say whether it detained anyone or found anything.
- **Further US alerts:** the embassy told Americans to monitor local media and enroll in the State Department's Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP) to receive updates.

## Quick answers

### What did the US Embassy in Moscow warn about?

It said it had credible information about a possible mass casualty event at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve on Oct. 31, and it advised people to avoid the area and crowds.

### Did an attack happen at Tsaritsyno?

None had been reported in the coverage reviewed for this explainer. Russian authorities temporarily closed the reserve and restricted access on Saturday.

### Did the US warn Russia before the Crocus City Hall attack?

Yes. The embassy issued a public alert on March 7, 2024, and US officials said they shared information with Russian security services before the March 22 attack.
