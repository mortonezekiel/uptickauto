## What happened

Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for the U.S. Senate in Michigan on Sunday, Nov. 1, two days before Election Day. That morning she sat with Rogers and his wife at a service at Perfecting Church in Detroit, while other Democrats gathered at a separate Detroit church to rally behind their nominee, Abdul El-Sayed, [Bridge Michigan reports](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-politics/haley-stevens-joins-mike-rogers-in-detroit-bucking-abdul-el-sayed-in-senate-race/).

In a video announcing the decision, Stevens said El-Sayed had made it plain she was no longer welcome in his campaign, and that this was why she was backing Rogers, according to [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/mike-rogers-wins-haley-stevens-backing-in-blow-to-democrats-senate-hopes-12646957). "We can't stand with hate," she said, pointing to her running dispute with El-Sayed over U.S. support for Israel's government, [BridgeDetroit reports](https://www.bridgedetroit.com/haley-stevens-endorses-mike-rogers-bucking-abdul-el-sayed-in-michigan-senate-race/).

The result: a sitting Democratic member of Congress is now publicly backing the Republican in her own state's Senate race, with about 48 hours left.

## How the rift grew

Stevens and El-Sayed fought a close primary in August. El-Sayed, a former Michigan public health official, won 48.4% to 47.6% under certified results, a gap of fewer than 14,000 votes, Newsweek reports. Stevens had much of the party establishment behind her, including the Senate Democrats' campaign arm, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/haley-stevens-mike-rogers-together-church-abdul-el-sayed-michigan-senate/). El-Sayed drew support from leading progressives. Israel and the war in Gaza split their supporters.

After losing, Stevens kept her promise to endorse the nominee. The break came in the campaign's last week:

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Last week of October | Stevens presses El-Sayed to denounce a third-party ad that ties U.S. military aid to Israel to grocery prices. She calls it antisemitic. |
| Same week | Stevens tells CNN she will still vote for El-Sayed. Her fiancé, Jason Turkish, says he will vote for Rogers. |
| Friday, Oct. 30 | Rogers praises Stevens on X for raising Jewish voters' concerns. |
| Saturday, Oct. 31 | Stevens says she is glad El-Sayed disagrees with the ad. El-Sayed says he would rather not distract from the campaign's main issues. |
| Sunday, Nov. 1 | A planned unity event with El-Sayed, Stevens and Rep. Jim Clyburn falls apart. Stevens joins Rogers at church and endorses him. |

The Anti-Defamation League has also criticized the ad, Newsweek reports. El-Sayed says he did not make or approve it and disagrees with what it says, and he has accused Stevens of reopening a primary that ended three months ago, CBS News reports. Rep. Debbie Dingell, who helped organize the unity event, told the Associated Press she was the one who asked Stevens to stay away because the situation had become "way too distracting," Newsweek reports.

## Who says what

- **Rogers** called her decision "courage in politics" and said he was glad not to be running against her, according to BridgeDetroit.
- **El-Sayed** said some people will always have different opinions and that voters are free to weigh other issues more heavily, Bridge Michigan reports.
- **Michigan Democratic Party Chair Curtis Hertel** said Stevens had abandoned what she claims to care about, and rejected efforts to divide the party.
- **Stevens' own office** took a hit: chief of staff Justin German resigned, said many other aides did too, and urged voters to back El-Sayed, BridgeDetroit reports.
- **Ronna McDaniel**, a former Republican national and Michigan party chair, praised Stevens for putting the state ahead of party.

## Why it matters for Senate control

The seat belongs to Democrat Gary Peters, who is retiring, so neither candidate is an incumbent. Republicans hold the Senate 53-47 and are defending 22 of the 35 seats up this year, according to Newsweek.

Here is the arithmetic. With Vice President JD Vance able to break 50-50 ties, Democrats need 51 seats, a net gain of four. If they hold all 13 of their seats on the ballot, flipping four Republican seats gets them there. If Rogers wins Michigan, Democrats start at 46 and would need to flip five.

That is why turnout in Detroit and among Black and Jewish voters is getting so much attention, Bridge Michigan reports. Stevens carried both Wayne County, which includes Detroit, and Oakland County in the primary. Early voting in Michigan was running more than 30% ahead of the same point in 2022, according to an AP analysis cited by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/31/us-midterms-3-days-to-go-what-latest-polls-say-as-early-voting-surges).

## What happens next

Polls close Tuesday, Nov. 3. Early voting in Michigan began Oct. 24, so many ballots were cast before Sunday's news. Whether the endorsement moves votes is something only the results can show. If El-Sayed wins, he would be the first Muslim U.S. senator, Bridge Michigan notes. ## Quick answers

### Who is Haley Stevens?

She is a Democratic U.S. representative from Michigan who ran for the open Senate seat this year and narrowly lost the August primary to Abdul El-Sayed.

### Why did Haley Stevens endorse Mike Rogers?

In her video, she said the nominee had shut her out of his campaign. She also pointed to their clash over Israel and a third-party ad she called antisemitic. El-Sayed says he did not make the ad.

### When is the Michigan Senate election?

Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. Early in-person voting began Oct. 24.
