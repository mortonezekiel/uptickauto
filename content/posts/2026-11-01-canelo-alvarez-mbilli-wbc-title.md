## What happened

Canelo Alvarez is a world champion again. The Mexican star beat Christian Mbilli by unanimous decision over 12 rounds in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, on Saturday, Oct. 31, and took back the WBC super middleweight title. The judges scored it 117-110, 116-111 and 115-112, [ESPN reports](https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/50341266/canelo-alvarez-wins-unanimous-decision-christian-mbilli).

It was the 36-year-old's first fight in 13 months. His last outing was a decision loss to Terence Crawford in Las Vegas in September 2025, which cost him the 168-pound belts he held. He had elbow surgery during the layoff.

For Mbilli, it was the first loss of his professional career, according to [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/canelo-alvarez-outboxes-christian-mbilli-001758231.html). He came in at 29-0-1 with 24 knockouts and was promoted to full WBC champion in January 2026, [Depor reports](https://depor.com/en/us-live/who-won-canelo-vs-christian-mbilli-results-and-official-scorecards-winners-boxing-nnda-nnrt-noticia/). Alvarez's record is now 64-3-2 with 39 knockouts.

## How the Canelo vs. Mbilli fight was won

Mbilli started as the busier fighter, pressing forward and throwing more in the early rounds. Alvarez's punches were the cleaner and harder ones, though, and he was comfortable enough by Round 3 to wave Mbilli in from the ropes.

The fifth and sixth rounds were close. Alvarez took the seventh with a four-punch combination and a right uppercut, and ESPN says Mbilli looked short of ideas by then. In Round 8, referee Hector Afu took a point from Mbilli for punches to the back of the head after Alvarez complained, a call the broadcast team questioned.

From there Mbilli fought more from the outside and was less effective. He opened up with a late flurry in the 12th, but it was too late to matter. CompuBox counted 132 power punches landed for Alvarez to 99 for Mbilli. Alvarez never seemed to chase a knockout, which ESPN notes has been a pattern for several years.

## Reading the scorecards

Boxing judges usually score each round 10-9 for the winner. If every round was scored that way, the three cards translate neatly into rounds won, once you account for Mbilli's lost point:

| Judge's card | Rounds for Alvarez | Rounds for Mbilli |
|---|---|---|
| 117-110 | 9 | 3 |
| 116-111 | 8 | 4 |
| 115-112 | 7 | 5 |

So even the closest card had Alvarez winning seven of 12 rounds. The point deduction widened each margin by one, but it didn't decide the fight: without it, every card would still have favored Alvarez.

## Why it matters

Alvarez has lost only three times as a professional, and this win came straight after the most recent one:

| Year | Loss | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | Floyd Mayweather Jr. | Las Vegas |
| 2022 | Dmitry Bivol | Las Vegas |
| 2025 | Terence Crawford | Las Vegas |

The win also answers questions about his age and long layoff. Yahoo Sports notes he won his first world title 16 years ago. After the fight Alvarez said, "This feels like my first title," and he credited trainer Eddy Reynoso's game plan.

The fight came close to being thrown into doubt. Strikes on Riyadh in October raised security concerns, and Saudi Arabia's entertainment authority chief Turki Alalshikh said the bout would go ahead with Alvarez's agreement, [The National reported](https://www.thenationalnews.com/sport/combat-sports/2026/10/12/canelo-alvarez-fears-nothing-and-will-fight-in-riyadh-despite-security-concerns/).

## The rest of the card

The event was billed as "Mexico vs the Rest of the World."

- **Jaime Munguia** kept his WBA super middleweight title with a unanimous decision over Nikita Zon (116-112, 117-111, 117-111).
- **Rafael Espinoza** kept the WBO featherweight title when Ricardo Blandon's corner stopped the fight after a cut in Round 3.
- **Reito Tsutsumi** stopped Raymundo Rojas in Round 2 after two knockdowns.
- **Carl Martin** beat David Picasso on the cards (94-94, 95-94, 96-93).

A vacant IBF junior welterweight title fight between Lindolfo Delgado and Arthur Biyarslanov did not take place.

## What happens next

Alvarez said he will rest for a couple of weeks and keep training, and he did not name an opponent. He has talked about having a few more fights and ending his career with one last bout in Mexico. ESPN lists the options most often mentioned:

- **Hamzah Sheeraz** (24-0-1, 19 KOs), a hard-hitting super middleweight.
- **A Bivol rematch** with the light heavyweight champion (25-1, 12 KOs), avenging the 2022 loss.
- **David Benavidez** (32-0, 26 KOs), the WBC light heavyweight champion.
