## What happened

The Eagle's Nest, the mountain house known in German as the Kehlsteinhaus, was largely destroyed by fire overnight from Friday, Oct. 23, into Saturday. Police told the [Associated Press](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/world/fire-devastates/CYUASR2THM7TJJNIIP2CHXT5D4/) that the building above Berchtesgaden, in the Bavarian Alps, had burned down almost completely, and that it was not yet clear whether anything could be saved.

No one was hurt. The building had already closed for the day, and searchers found nobody inside. About 200 firefighters and emergency workers were sent up the mountain, [Reuters reported](https://www.rte.ie/news/world/2026/1024/1596690-fire-engulfs-eagles-nest-hitlers-mountain-retreat/), among them mountain rescuers, Red Cross staff and police.

Police detained one person soon after the fire began. They have not said whether that person had anything to do with it. A police spokesman, Daniel Katz, said investigators are looking into suspected arson, according to Austria's [ORF](https://orf.at/stories/3446029/). The cause is officially undetermined.

The location made the job harder. The house stands at roughly 1,834 meters (6,017 feet), at the top of a narrow road that is closed to private cars. Heavy fire engines struggle on it, and crews could only go in wearing heavy breathing equipment, a police spokeswoman told ORF.

## Why the Eagle's Nest matters

The Kehlsteinhaus was built in 1937-38 at the urging of Martin Bormann, one of Adolf Hitler's closest aides, and paid for by the Nazi Party. It was designed by architect Roderich Fick and cost about 30 million Reichsmarks, [Salzburg24](https://www.salzburg24.at/news/salzburg/grenznah/hitlers-ehemaliges-eagles-nest-am-obersalzberg-brennt-lichterloh-erste-festnahme-art-342356) reports.

It stood inside the fenced-off Obersalzberg compound where the Nazi leadership had homes. Two points often get mixed up:

- **It was not Hitler's house.** His residence on the mountain was the Berghof, a separate building lower down. The Kehlsteinhaus was a place to receive and impress diplomats and guests.
- **It was not a birthday present.** It was formally opened on April 20, 1939, Hitler's 50th birthday, but both Salzburg24 and the [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kehlsteinhaus) say it wasn't meant as a gift. Hitler went there only rarely; Salzburg24 links that to his fear of heights. Other Nazi officials used it more often.

English speakers mostly know it as the "Eagle's Nest," the name used for it in American war films, [Der Spiegel](https://ua.news/en/world/u-nimechchini-gorit-kelshtainkhaus-na-oberzaltsberzi-der-spiegel) notes.

In April 1945, Allied bombers wrecked much of the Obersalzberg, but the Kehlsteinhaus came through almost untouched. It was also spared a planned demolition after the war. Bavaria owns it today, and it is a protected monument. It has served visitors as a mountain restaurant for decades, with an exhibition by Germany's Institute of Contemporary History (Institut für Zeitgeschichte) explaining its role in the Nazi dictatorship.

| Responders on the night | About 200 |

The arithmetic shows how busy the site is. A season from mid-May to Oct. 31 runs about 170 days, so 350,000 visitors works out to roughly 2,000 people a day, all of them arriving by special bus, on foot or by bike. The fire came in the final days of the 2026 season.

## What happens next

The criminal police in Traunstein have taken over the case under the direction of prosecutors. Police said they would give an update on Saturday, and the investigation into the person they detained is continuing. Until investigators report, Uptick isn't speculating about how the fire started.

The damage assessment is still pending, so no one has said yet whether the building can be restored or whether the site can reopen when the season would normally start in May.

## Quick answers

### Where is the Eagle's Nest?

It sits on the Kehlstein ridge above the Obersalzberg, near the town of Berchtesgaden in southeastern Bavaria, close to the border with Austria.

### Did Hitler live at the Eagle's Nest?

No. His mountain home was the Berghof, elsewhere on the Obersalzberg. The Kehlsteinhaus was a guest and reception house that he visited only occasionally.

### Was anyone hurt in the fire?

No injuries were reported. The building was closed when the fire started, and firefighters found no one inside.
