## What happened

Patrick Stewart, the British stage actor who became a global star as Capt. Jean-Luc Picard on *Star Trek* and Professor Charles Xavier in the *X-Men* films, has died. He was 86. He died in Los Angeles, at home, on Wednesday, Oct. 28, 2026, according to a family statement posted to his social media accounts and reported by [Yahoo Entertainment](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/live/patrick-stewart-tributes-hollywood-celebrates-the-life-and-legacy-of-the-star-trek-star-following-his-death-at-86-204932222.html).

The statement said Stewart had Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia, and called his illness "a brief and poignant journey." It said his wife, Sunny Ozell, was caring for him and that family and friends were with him at the end. His longtime spokeswoman, Kelly Bush Novak, confirmed the details to [Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/patrick-stewart-legendary-star-trek-x-men-actor-dies-86). His representatives asked people who want to honor him to donate to an Alzheimer's research fund at Johns Hopkins and to caregiver programs run by the Alzheimer's Foundation of America.

## Why everyone is talking about it

Few actors were attached to two franchises this large. Stewart's *Star Trek* work alone covered more than 35 years, and he was still on screen in 2026. Co-stars responded within hours. Jonathan Frakes, who played Will Riker, shared the family's notice, [TVLine reports](https://www.tvline.com/2301727/patrick-stewart-star-trek-the-next-generation-actor-death-tributes/). William Shatner, the original Enterprise captain, called him "a magnificent actor." Hugh Jackman, his *X-Men* partner as Wolverine, said he would miss him "all of my days." Ian McKellen, a friend since their Royal Shakespeare Company years who officiated Stewart's wedding, also paid tribute. By Oct. 30, fans had left flowers at his star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, [Euronews reported](https://www.euronews.com/video/2026/10/30/latest-news-bulletin-october-30th-2026-midday).

Stewart grew up in Mirfield, Yorkshire, and left school at 15. He trained at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School before the RSC years. A *Next Generation* producer cast him after seeing him read Shakespeare at a lecture at UCLA, Fox News reports. That show ran seven seasons and led to four films. He also made comedy part of his range, from *Robin Hood: Men in Tights* to *Extras* and the voice of Avery Bullock on *American Dad!* He published a memoir, *Making It So*, in 2023, and he served as chancellor of the University of Huddersfield near his hometown.

He has one more major release ahead. He reprises Professor X in *Avengers: Doomsday*, which opens Dec. 18, according to Yahoo Entertainment. He is survived by his wife and his two children, Daniel and Sophia.

## What Lewy body dementia is

"Dementia" isn't a single disease. It's a name for a group of symptoms, such as memory loss, confusion and changes in mood, that several brain diseases can cause, [Alzheimer's Research UK explains](https://www.alzheimersresearchuk.org/news/sir-patrick-stewart-dies-with-alzheimers-disease-and-lewy-body-dementia/). When someone has more than one of those diseases at once, as Stewart's family described, doctors call it mixed dementia.

The two diseases work in different ways:

| | Alzheimer's disease | Dementia with Lewy bodies |
|---|---|---|
| Share of dementia cases | About 60% | About 10% |
| Protein involved | Amyloid and tau | Alpha-synuclein |
| Typical early signs | Memory and planning problems | Swings in alertness, visual hallucinations, movement trouble |

The Lewy-body figure means roughly 1 in 10 people with dementia has that form, about 100,000 people in the U.K., the charity estimates. In Lewy body disease, a protein called alpha-synuclein builds up in clumps inside nerve cells. Those clumps are the "Lewy bodies" in the name. The [National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/dementia-lewy-bodies) lists acting out dreams during sleep, stiffness, tremor and falls among other signs, and notes that memory loss tends to come later than in Alzheimer's.

That overlap makes diagnosis hard. NINDS says early symptoms are often mistaken for Alzheimer's, no single test confirms the disease, and certainty only comes from examining the brain after death. There is no cure; treatment targets the symptoms. From diagnosis, people live an average of five to seven years, with a range of two to 20, the agency says. It usually starts at 50 or older and affects slightly more men than women. Public awareness grew after Robin Williams, who was found after his death in 2014 to have had the disease. His daughter, Zelda Williams, was among those paying tribute to Stewart this week.
